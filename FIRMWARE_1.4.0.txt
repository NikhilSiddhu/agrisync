Firmware 1.4.0 — cellular, and a way home without it
====================================================

    scp ~/Downloads/agrisync_fw140.zip agrisync@nikhil.local:~/
    cd ~/agrisync && unzip -o ~/agrisync_fw140.zip

*** THIS HAS NEVER BEEN COMPILED. ***

I could not build it here — the sandbox kills background processes and the
ESP32 toolchain is a 200 MB download. Structure is verified (balanced
preprocessor, balanced braces, every symbol present) but you will be the first
to run a compiler over it. Budget 20 minutes on Saturday for that, before you
touch the modem.

    cd ~/agrisync/firmware/synthetic_wifi
    sudo systemctl stop agrisync-serial-bridge
    export WIFI_SSID='your-wifi' WIFI_PASSWORD='your-wifi-password' PI_HOST='192.168.1.39'
    export DEVICE_API_KEY="$(grep '^DEVICE_API_KEY=' ~/agrisync/.env | cut -d= -f2)"
    export CELL_APN='jionet' CF_ACCESS_ID='x' CF_ACCESS_SECRET='x'
    pio run                       # compile only. Fix anything it finds.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
TRANSPORT, one flag in platformio.ini
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

    -DTRANSPORT=0    tethered to the Pi over USB      <- where you are now
    -DTRANSPORT=1    WiFi                              bench only
    -DTRANSPORT=2    A7670C cellular                   the field

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
CELLULAR (TRANSPORT=2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Wiring        ESP32 GPIO26 -> modem RXD
                ESP32 GPIO27 -> modem TXD
                common ground
                modem on its OWN supply, never the ESP32's 3V3

  Power         2 A bursts on transmit. At least 470 uF at the module, short
                thick leads. A sagging supply resets the modem mid-request and
                looks exactly like a firmware bug.

  Antenna       On before power. Every time. Transmitting into an open
                connector can destroy the amplifier.

  Flash first   Every reading is written to flash and only removed once the
                server confirms it. ~7000 readings, about 26 days at the
                5-minute field interval.

  Interval      5 minutes in cellular mode, 1 minute on the bench. Twelve
                samples an hour is ample for wet-hour accounting and it
                quadruples both battery life and storage.

  Build flags   CELL_APN            jionet (Jio) or airtelgprs.com (Airtel)
                CF_ACCESS_ID        Cloudflare service token client ID
                CF_ACCESS_SECRET    service token client secret
                MODEM_TRACE=1       every byte to and from the modem.
                                    Turn this on for the first bring-up.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
IF THE MODEM IS NOT READY ON MONDAY — deploy anyway
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Flash with TRANSPORT=2 regardless. With no working modem every POST fails,
  so every reading stays in flash. The box records for about 26 days
  unattended.

  To collect it, plug the board into the Pi (or a laptop) over USB:

      sudo systemctl start agrisync-serial-bridge
      journalctl -u agrisync-serial-bridge -f

  The bridge now sends 'D' on connect, the board replays its whole buffer as
  normal [payload] lines, and they land in MongoDB with their real timestamps.

  Then, once you have confirmed the count, send 'C' to clear it. Two steps on
  purpose — nothing deletes a fortnight of field data on a single keystroke.

  Serial commands:  D  dump buffer
                    C  clear it
                    ?  firmware version, buffered count, bytes used

  This means the box can go in the ground on Monday whether or not the
  cellular link works. Measurement starts with the crop, which is the part you
  cannot go back and recover.
